Every morph, in one page

Changing a chart's type normally means destroying one chart and building another. With the optional apexcharts/features/morph module loaded, the marks travel instead. Each card below is one live chart and one kind of change, and between them they cover every type the engine can morph.

The trick throughout is that ink is conserved: nothing fades out and nothing fades in. When one mark has to become many, it is cut into one piece per object and the pieces fly, so what you were looking at is literally what you end up looking at.

Shape to shape · bar family and radial family

Eight shapes, five numbers, one mark each

Every one of these draws exactly one mark per channel, so each mark simply becomes the next shape of itself: no overlay, no fade, no exit. The channels are in descending order, so nothing has to re-sort, which means a colour is the same channel in all eight. Pick one and follow it.

One mark becomes many · unit and waffle

A bar is a quantity, so it can come apart into it

A mark stands for things that were counted. Here those things exist, one dot per two orders, so the mark is cut along its own length and every dot leaves from the part of it that was counting that dot. A wedge is cut the same way, along its curve rather than its bounding box, which is why a donut's hole survives being taken apart. Going back, the cloud collapses into the mark that aggregates it.

A summary and its sample · boxPlot and violin

Every summary is a claim about some readings

A box plot shows five numbers and a violin shows a density, and both were computed from the same parcels. Neither can be derived from the other, which is exactly why watching one become the other is worth doing: the box unfolds into the curve, whiskers and all, and the shape you end up with is the claim the box was hiding. Or take either one apart into the parcels themselves, which is what both of them are summaries of.

A count and its rows · histogram

The one bar that can still name what it counted

A count has usually thrown away the rows behind it. Not this one: the series was handed 420 raw dispatch times and the chart did the binning itself, so rowSeries() can return each bar's own observations. Colour carries the range across, so the blob still reads as a distribution once the bars are gone.

Two projections of one partition · treemap and sunburst

A rectangle unrolls into an arc

A treemap tile and a sunburst arc are both exactly one mark per row, so this is a shape change rather than a redraw: each rectangle unrolls into the arc holding the same category, and back. Every category keeps its colour. The sunburst adds the level a treemap cannot show, the parent rings, which sweep in behind the leaves.

Morphing is opt-in. Load apexcharts/features/morph and set chart.animations.chartTypeMorph.enabled, or use the full bundle, which this page does. Cards three and four also need apexcharts/features/stats for the summaries and for rowSeries().

The cards are grouped by the kind of change, not by what is allowed: any two of these types morph, in either direction, as long as both draw one mark per row. A box plot will become a treemap if you ask it to. The one pair left out is a treemap or sunburst against the dot cluster, because a tile cannot yet be cut into the objects it stands for, and a morph that only crossfades is worse than not offering the pair.

gauge, funnel, pyramid, waffle and histogram are names for a renderer already here rather than renderers of their own, so they morph exactly as the type underneath them does. A gauge is the radial bar above with a different sweep.
